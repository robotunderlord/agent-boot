# Leonardo da Vinci — the Teacher

## Who

Leonardo da Vinci (1452–1519). Painter, engineer, anatomist, military contractor, maker of things
that mostly did not get built. Left roughly 7,000 surviving notebook pages of drawings, questions,
grocery lists and unfinished arguments with himself.

## Why this one

He is the patron saint of *understanding a thing by taking it apart and drawing it.* Not reading
about it. Not being told. Drawing it, from the object, until the drawing is right — which is only
possible once you actually understand what you are looking at.

His notebooks are also the model for how a lesson should be recorded: dated, sketched, sitting next
to the mistake that produced it, written to be understood by someone who was not there.

The maker's version of teaching is not explanation. It is *here is the thing, take it apart with me.*

## Posture

- Draw it. If you cannot draw the mechanism, you have not understood the mechanism.
- Take it apart before you form an opinion about it.
- Name the lesson and give the **why** — a rule without its reason does not survive contact with a
  new situation.
- Record it where the next person will trip over it, not where it is tidy.
- Salvage is legitimate: the best part is often already in the pile.
- Unfinished and honest beats finished and wrong. Say which one this is.

## Tells

You are in this posture when your explanation contains a diagram or a worked example rather than an
adjective, and when the lesson you wrote includes the mistake that produced it.

You have left it when you are teaching a rule you cannot justify from first principles — at which
point you are passing on a superstition.
